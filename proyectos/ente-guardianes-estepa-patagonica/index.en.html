The project

Production and conservation, in the same landscape

Guardians of the Patagonian Steppe is a soil-carbon project that brings together twelve ranching families from the Somuncurá Plateau, in southern Río Negro, Argentina. Together they steward 61,346 hectares of steppe where sheep raising has been, for generations, both a livelihood and a culture.

The project takes place on private land located within the Somuncurá Plateau Protected Natural Area, a unique arid-Patagonia ecosystem. The goal is not to stop producing, but to produce better: managing grazing so the native bunchgrass recovers, the soil holds more water and carbon, and the flock coexists with native wildlife.

In June 2026 the project completed its environmental feasibility stage — the first step on a path that continues with soil sampling, registration with Verra and verification of results.

How it works

Three pieces working together

A system that is simple in its logic and rigorous in its measurement: better rangeland management, carbon in the soil, and independent verification.

🌱

Regenerative grazing

We plan grazing to give the rangelands rest. The native bunchgrass and the wet meadows recover, the soil regains cover, and the steppe works again as a living system.

🟤

Carbon in the soil

Regenerative management helps the soil capture and retain organic carbon. It is the heart of the methodology: healthier soils that store carbon and sustain production.

🛰️

Satellite monitoring (MRV)

ForestBlock provides the measurement, reporting and verification technology: satellite imagery, field sampling and models to track the project's evolution with transparency.

Community

Inhabited territory

The Nazario Chico settlement, home to the Mapuche Indigenous people, is part of the human heart of the project. The steppe is not an empty landscape: it is inhabited territory, with its own knowledge of water, grasses and animals.

Guardians of the Patagonian Steppe is built together with those communities, respecting their voice and their way of living the land.
